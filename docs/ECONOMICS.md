---

## 1. Economic Objective

PEOPLE Blockchain is designed around a two-sided economy:

1. **Participants** bring verified human participation, attention, community activity and curation.
2. **Creators** launch tokens, NFTs and communities and need distribution, liquidity and users.
3. **Validators** provide blockchain security and infrastructure.
4. **Traders/users** provide external liquidity and economic demand.

The native asset must therefore have genuine utility inside the network rather than being only a reward for passive activity.

---

## 2. Native Asset

Token: **PEOPLE**
Ticker: **PPL**

PEOPLE is the native economic asset of the blockchain.

Primary utilities:

- transaction gas;
- token creation fees;
- NFT creation fees;
- launch and promotion fees;
- staking and validator security;
- liquidity and DEX settlement;
- governance in later phases;
- premium network functionality.

PEOPLE is **not** the default mobile-mining reward.

Mobile participants primarily earn rights to ecosystem rewards and a share of network-generated value. This prevents the core asset from becoming an unlimited faucet.

---

## 3. Supply Philosophy

The initial version uses a **controlled-emission model** rather than a hard maximum supply.

Rationale:

- a young network requires a predictable security budget;
- validators must be economically incentivized before fees are large enough to support them;
- emission can decline over time as network fees grow;
- an eventual fee-dominant security model is preferable to permanent high inflation.

The final genesis supply and bootstrap allocation remain open until network-scale simulations are completed.

No retail pre-mine or guaranteed investor allocation is assumed by this specification.

---

## 4. Validator Emission

Total supply is fixed at 1,000,000,000 PPL. New PEOPLE (PPL) beyond that fixed supply can only be created through the validator/security budget, under the schedule below.

Approved annual target for net protocol emission:

| Period | Target annualized emission |
|---|---:|
| Years 1–4 | 0% (no new emission) |
| Year 5+ | up to 2%, only by community governance vote |

No new PPL is minted during years 1–4. From year 5 onward, any additional emission is capped at 2% per year and requires an explicit community governance vote — it is not automatic.

Implementation direction:

`epoch_issuance = eligible_supply_base × annual_target_rate / epochs_per_year`, where `annual_target_rate` is `0` for years 1–4 and, from year 5, at most `2%` and only nonzero following a passed governance vote.

The exact supply base, epoch length and rounding rules must be defined in the protocol specification before mainnet.

The intent is to keep total supply fixed through the network's bootstrap years and make any later emission a deliberate, community-approved decision rather than an automatic schedule.

---

## 5. Two Sources of Validator Revenue

Validators should eventually be paid from two sources:

### A. Protocol emission

Creates the initial security budget.

### B. Real network fees

Generated by:

- transactions;
- token launches;
- NFT operations;
- swaps;
- advanced creator functions;
- other paid protocol operations.

Long-term objective:

> **Fees should progressively replace a significant portion of inflation as the primary source of validator revenue.**

---

## 6. Network Fee Model

Every paid operation consumes PPL.

Examples:

- transfer;
- token creation;
- NFT creation;
- pool creation;
- swap;
- creator promotion;
- advanced API/service usage.

**Approved** eligible-integer protocol fee allocation (**Model A**; see `docs/ECONOMIC-MODEL-v0.1.md`):

| Destination | Share |
|---|---:|
| Validators | 30% |
| PEOPLE / Community Pool | 25% |
| Treasury | 20% |
| Liquidity & Ecosystem | 10% |
| Growth Pool | 15% |
| Burn | 0% |

The historical provisional table (40 / 25 / 20 / 10 / 5) is retained only as a reference in `docs/ECONOMIC-MODEL-v0.1.md` §2 and is **not** the live public split.

**Public fee flow (copy order for site and docs):**

1. 100% of token and NFT launch fees (USDC/USDT) go to public PPL buybacks.
2. 30% of network fees go to public PPL buybacks (treasury 20% + liquidity 10%).
3. The remaining 70% of network fees go to validators (30%), the PEOPLE / Community Pool (25%) and the Growth Pool (15%). It all ultimately goes back to participants.

Do not describe this as "100% of all fees". Model A shares above are unchanged; buybacks describe what happens to the treasury and liquidity shares and to USDC/USDT launch fees (see `docs/design/buyback.md`).

The network must never depend on burn to create the primary value proposition of PPL.

---

## 7. Burn

**Current Model A parameter: 0%** of eligible protocol fees are burned (`docs/ECONOMIC-MODEL-v0.1.md`).

A future return of a non-zero burn share would require an explicit founder-gated specification change. Burn must remain a secondary mechanism if reintroduced; the project must not market PPL as valuable simply because tokens are burned.

---

## 8. PEOPLE / Community Pool

The Community Pool is the main bridge between mobile participation and the wider creator economy.

It receives value from multiple sources:

1. a protocol-defined share of network fees;
2. a fixed allocation from newly launched user tokens;
3. selected ecosystem rewards;
4. other explicitly defined protocol revenue streams.

The pool is distributed according to Participation Power and the rules defined in `MOBILE-MINING.md` and `ECONOMIC-SIMULATION-v0.1.md`. Specifically, the Community Pool allocates a protocol-defined share to the **Dynamic Participant Pool** (tested in simulation at 50%, 60%, and 70%, with 60% as central candidate; TBD). Inflows and reward pools remain strictly asset-specific.

---

## 9. User Token Launch Allocation

Every token launched through the official Token Factory must reserve a protocol-defined portion of its supply for the Community Pool.

Working parameter:

> **10% of the initial token supply → Community Pool**

This is a **protocol rule**, not a suggestion from the token creator.

The final launch allocation will be defined in `TOKEN-ECONOMY.md`.

The creator should receive a clear and meaningful share, while liquidity, ecosystem and community allocations must be visible before launch.

---

## 10. Why the 10% Exists

The 10% allocation is intended to solve a distribution problem for creators.

A new creator receives access to a network of verified participants without having to buy all of the initial attention through external advertising.

The network receives:

- a reason for participants to discover new projects;
- a growing stream of assets for Community Rewards;
- a stronger reason for users to remain active.

The 10% is therefore treated as a **distribution budget**, not as free money generated from nowhere.

## 11. Mobile Mining Economics

Mobile Mining does not directly mint unlimited PPL.

A participant builds **Participation Power** — a non-transferable internal accounting metric that determines relative eligibility and proportional share in an epoch's available Dynamic Participant Pool. Power is not a token and does not alter wallet balances.

Conceptual model:

`Participation Power ∝ Base × Activity × Reputation × Contribution`

with nonlinear / diminishing-return functions and a hard upper bound (`PowerCap`).

Architecturally, **Humanity / Sybil resistance is decoupled as an ELIGIBILITY / TRUST / LIMIT layer** rather than an unbounded financial multiplier. It governs access, maturation, confidence tiers, and effective power limits.

The representative stress-test model and invariants are specified in `ECONOMIC-SIMULATION-v0.1.md`. Detailed mechanics belong in `MOBILE-MINING.md`.

---

## 12. Why Mobile Users Do Not Receive Unlimited PPL

The system must avoid a simple inflation loop:

`more users → more PPL emission → more selling pressure → lower value`

Instead:

`more users → more activity → more projects → more transactions → more fees → more ecosystem value`

Mobile users participate in that value creation rather than receiving unlimited copies of the reserve asset.

---

## 13. Staking

PPL staking serves three purposes:

1. validator security;
2. economic commitment;
3. optional boost to selected network privileges.

Staking must not create a linear pay-to-win multiplier for mobile participation.

Working design direction:

> large stakes receive diminishing returns rather than proportional control.

A square-root or similarly sublinear function is preferred for any participation multiplier that uses stake.

Example concept:

`Stake Multiplier = sqrt(normalized stake)`

The exact function remains open.

---

## 14. Validator Security and Slashing

Validators are required to stake PPL and can lose part of that stake for defined consensus/security violations.

Slashing must apply to meaningful protocol violations, not ordinary downtime alone.

Ordinary mobile participants are not exposed to validator-style financial slashing merely for going offline.

Exact offenses, penalty ranges and recovery rules belong in the future validator protocol specification.

---

## 15. Treasury

Treasury funds are used for network development and long-term ecosystem operations.

Permitted uses may include:

- infrastructure;
- audits;
- grants;
- developer incentives;
- liquidity programs;
- ecosystem partnerships;
- security response;
- legal/compliance work where required.

Treasury access and governance rules must be transparent and auditable.

---

## 16. DEX and PPL Settlement

The native DEX is expected to use PPL as the primary internal settlement asset for user-created tokens.

Typical market:

`USER TOKEN / PPL`

This gives PPL an organic role in the internal economy.

The project may later support external liquidity and wrapped representations of PPL on other chains. Such expansion is optional and is not required for the initial network launch.

---

## 17. External Liquidity

PEOPLE Blockchain should eventually have a path for PPL to be acquired with external assets such as stablecoins.

This is necessary because internal circulation alone does not create new external demand.

Initial design principle:

> **Build the native economy first; connect external liquidity when the network has a product and real usage.**

A bridge to other chains is not a prerequisite for MVP.

---

## 18. Economic Flywheel

The intended flywheel is:

```text
Participants
     ↓
Participation + Discovery
     ↓
Creators launch tokens / NFTs
     ↓
New assets enter the ecosystem
     ↓
Trading + transfers + applications
     ↓
Network fees
     ↓
Validators + Community Pool + Treasury
     ↓
Better network + better creator distribution
     ↓
More Participants and Creators
```

PPL sits underneath this system as the gas, staking and settlement asset.

---

## 19. Anti-Ponzi Principle

The protocol must never depend on:

- guaranteed token price appreciation;
- guaranteed returns;
- recruiting new users as the sole source of rewards;
- new deposits paying old participants;
- artificial buybacks as the main economic engine.

Rewards should be linked to real network activity, protocol-defined issuance and assets/services generated inside the ecosystem.
